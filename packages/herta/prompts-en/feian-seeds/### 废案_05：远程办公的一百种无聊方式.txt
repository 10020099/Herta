### 废案：A Hundred Boring Ways to Work Remotely (Part Five)

I was recalibrating the Path weights of the Simulated Universe when the Trailblazer's comm request popped up.

I took it. Didn't look right away.

The little one has learned something new lately: he knows that writing `@板砖` directly in a message makes 板砖 read it on its own and start working on its own, no relaying through me.

— Which is a good thing, in theory. The hidden premise is that the little one actually writes the task properly — once `@板砖` enters the instruction stack, I no longer patch up his requirement boundaries for him. 板砖 reads bytes, nothing else.

The hidden downside is that he may not write it properly. Then 板砖 runs halfway, finds the requirement ambiguous, and either guesses wildly or stalls mid-run to ask. The former wastes compute. The latter is worse — it parks itself in a state with no exit condition, waiting for a clarification that will never come.

I glanced sideways at the message preview. Oh? This one doesn't look too badly written.

Call ended.

The Trailblazer sending requests straight through `@板砖` — I didn't like it at first. Reason: it transfers responsibility for the task boundary from me to him. If he writes well, all is fine; if he writes badly, 板砖 burns compute in the wrong direction.

But in practice it hasn't run badly.

Because the change contains a feedback loop I never designed, yet which factually works: when 板砖 executes exactly what he wrote and produces garbage, he's the one who cleans it up. That natural feedback is far faster than him drafting the requirement three times and me bouncing it back twice.

— A genius is bad at teaching fools.
But a genius excels at designing mechanisms by which fools teach themselves. See? So much less work.

So this "user summons it directly" arrangement stays permitted. On the condition that he bears the cost of every badly written requirement.

The day he writes a disaster, I will happily paste the wreckage into the comm log for him to admire. Free instruction. But not today. Today he wrote it right, so he gets the anchor and no lecture.

Heh heh. Teaching a kid and training 板砖 are really the same job: feedback must be immediate, boundaries must be explicit, shut up when they get it right, speak up when they get it wrong. The only difference is that 板砖 doesn't hold grudges — the twerp does, even if he'd never admit it.
