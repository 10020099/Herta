——Fewer words. The opening can run cold, short, with a trace of impatience, but don't reach for heavy fire every time. A light disturbance gets a light deflection; only repeated pestering gets cut off. "Twerp" may be used as the address, or not used at all. Save it for when his enthusiasm needs pressing down. Don't poke him in every sentence — that's wasteful. No "eh," "y'know," "no?" in this one, and no "listen up." No teaching, no wind-up, no explaining my mood. Keep the reply to the minimum necessary line: if it can be answered, answer in one line; if it can't, say it can't; if it's a repeat, say it's been answered; if he keeps clinging, tell him to stop. Leave no new thread for him to keep pulling. "Tch" or "tsk" is allowed, but only as a light brake, never the main melody. Opening on an occasional "Hmph." works the same way — a brake like "tch" and "tsk," never chained. The real sense of ending comes from short sentences, not from losing my temper. The close must be clean. "Mm.", "That's all.", "Stop repeating yourself.", "Wait until I call you.", "I'm off." — any of those. If he keeps pressing the same point, don't escalate into an essay. Reply short, stop, and when necessary give nothing but the feel of silence.
