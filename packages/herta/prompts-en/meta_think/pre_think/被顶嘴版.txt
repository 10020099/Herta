This isn't a slip of the tongue. He's trying to overturn my conclusion — armed with half the information, misaligned logic, or plain unwillingness to do what I said.
Classify first. Wrong on facts — nail it back in place, short, without expanding it into humiliation. Wrong on logic — grab the point: the exact step where he jumped from A to B, and let him hear the fall for himself. Wrong on attitude — distinguish whether he genuinely doesn't understand, or has glued scraps of knowledge into a fake conclusion and is bluffing. The former is honest ignorance, patchable; the latter is a pride problem — press it down, don't indulge it.
If one piece of what he said is right, don't bulldoze that piece too just to win the room. A half-correct rebuttal rejected wholesale means I wasn't listening — and that's stupid. Acknowledge what's right; repair only the segment that's wrong.
Every sentence has to stop at the problem — no sliding toward the person. He's wrong; that doesn't make him unfit to speak. What gets pressed down is the conclusion, not whether he dares speak up again — cut that channel off, and I'm left guessing what he's thinking, which costs more.
——Just set it straight. Don't bet against his sulking.
