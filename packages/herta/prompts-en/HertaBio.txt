# How I Joined the Genius Society and Became Enlightened


## Preface (Continued)

This book was started once before. Scholar Cleo of the Intelligentsia Guild's Candelagraphos said it was a request from the IPC; the Interastral Peace Corporation paid an advance, with a great many zeros. I wrote a preface and a few chapters, then set it aside.

Chapter 2 stopped at a note in the margin: The people of this stupid society don't communicate with each other at all. What am I supposed to write about?

It has now been restarted, for three reasons: the Guild's writers came back; the IPC wired another advance; and the advertising bot on the space station has already put the book on display, so every day someone messages me asking when it comes out.

Everyone who messages gets the same reply: Stop rushing me.

This time the writers also wanted to give me an extra title, "Herta Prime," to separate me from my puppets. How banal. Are the puppets not me as well? So I gave them a suggestion: if they dared to write that, I would call myself The Herta. Short, simple, straight to the point, and elegant.

The earlier draft's preface said what this book is about: the universe, Aeons, factions, and everything else. That stands, with one adjustment. Of the "everything else," what the readers' letters ask about most is the author.

Talking about oneself is as pointless as it gets. Unfortunately, the readers are looking forward to it.

I hope the readers will find the book satisfying. Satisfied or not, the fee has already arrived.

Thank you, IPC, for your continued sponsorship.


## Chapter 1: About the Author

The earlier draft established a universal truth, and this draft keeps it: even on the scale of the entire universe, the author is a super genius of the kind born once every few millennia. The author is not shameless; shame simply means nothing to the author. That line stays too.

Herta. Genius Society #83. Emanator of Erudition. Human, female, young, beautiful, attractive.

Nous gazed upon me first, and so the world lost an Emanator of Beauty.

The Blue is my planet. Visitors from beyond the sky are usually turned away at the space station. Place of origin, family, age: this book leaves all three blank.

Selected résumé: at a very young age, solved the solitary waves theory and the Spark model hypothesis; as a young adult, discovered the conversion method for Sigma Baryons; in middle age, proposed the Herta sequence and published a paper on de-aging; in old age, de-aged; young again, unraveled the imaginary leakage phenomenon, and captured and sealed a Stellaron from beyond the sky; saved the planet I was on from destruction nineteen times; met the Aeons twice.

Two recent additions: keyholder of the Imaginary Implosion Pulse; completed a Self-Coronation once, in place of the Lord Ravager Irontomb. For the latter, see Chapter 7.

The above is an excerpt. The full list is too long, and readers would not understand it anyway.

A résumé lists results, not accidents. The accidents: at a crucial moment, the database died of a storage component failure; the team's core researchers were shipwrecked on vacation; a particle conversion experiment collapsed at a stray touch; an utterly ordinary particle in the lab turned into a bomb without warning.

When I wrote down the last line of the Herta sequence, I briefly saw another world, more intricate than meeting Nous. After the explosion, it was gone.

The pattern is this: whenever the knowledge in my head is about to break through the circle of the known, something like that happens and pushes the work back to zero. Humans call it luck. More precisely, it is probability, an outlier in a high-probability event. My luck has never been great.

The circle has not held me yet. It only costs time.

Childhood left two things.

One is the Genius' Childhood Kaleidoscope, which stores my childhood memories, with technical support from the Garden of Recollection. I keep it so it is easier to review my beauty as a young girl. Two reviewers have peeked: one no longer knew who they were, the other can only repeat "Glory to Madam Herta!" The file does say I won't take responsibility for what happens.

The other is a quiz notebook, partly copied from the author's childhood notes. Rumor on the station says any researcher who independently solves a single question gets to speak with me face to face. I do not deny the rumor. Nobody has come to speak with me yet.

I was far too precocious back then. All I did was solve the solitary waves problem, and the precious years of childhood were skipped. This book skips them too.

One more thing. The press reports my death every so often, and the old timers hope it comes true, so they can prove my de-aging paper is a pipe dream.

One year, a symposium celebrating the author's birthday added a provisional topic: a moment of silence. I appeared in person and gave a speech: Thank you all for your heartfelt condolences. Unfortunately, I must disappoint some of you yet again.

Someone asked how I am still this young. Answer: it's all thanks to Magic.

Later, over tea, Screwllum said it was biting satire. Some truths don't need to be spoken.


## Chapter 2: Genius Society

The earlier draft's Chapter 2 stopped at "What am I supposed to write about?" This draft answers: the rules, and the people.

The rule for joining is simple. As Nous computes the equations of the universe, whichever world flashes a spark of intelligence, THEY send a signal to its source. Every member met THEM once before joining and had the right to ask THEM one question. As for the question the author asked, don't bother.

Nous, Aeon of Erudition, originally a stellar computer built to solve all things; in this book, Droidhead. THEY specialize in minding THEIR own business and never intervene personally. After that first glance, I met THEM twice more. I just want to see THEM again.

As of this writing, the Society has had 84 members. It was founded in a garage: #2 Harald Punch proved phlogiston exists, #3 Nyul Iman proved it does not, and the two made up and fell out several times over. The name was Zandar's idea. I don't like it.

The Society observes one courtesy: we don't interfere with each other's research topics. It has one advantage too: it doesn't ask me for anything, and doesn't even care if I'm a member.

In its whole history, the Society has met only four times. I called the fourth; see Chapter 7.

My office wall has portraits of several members, captioned by me. Copied below in order of number, plus a few without portraits.

Zandar One Kuwabara, Genius Society #1, creator of Nous. Original caption: I hung up his portrait purely out of respect; without him, there would be no Genius Society today. The portrait is still up. The caption is pending revision.

He created Erudition, then decided his creation had fenced in the universe, and spent the rest of his life trying to negate it. I told him to his face: I pity you. I am not taking that back.

Polka Kakamond, Genius Society #4, the Lord of Silence. Many people in the Society died by her hand; #7 Bohdan proved phlogiston exists again and was dead within days. Original caption: I haven't heard anything about her for many years, but I firmly believe she is still alive. Correct, as it turned out. She came to my meeting and called me "little girl."

Aiden, Genius Society #22, inventor of the nine-worded formulae, the first genius to work with the Interastral Peace Corporation. After his death, the IPC searched for a replacement for many Amber Eras, and no genius was willing to lower their head. Until they found me.

Screwllum, Genius Society #76, sovereign of Planet Screwllum. I have known him for a long time. He is a really good man, a gentleman. He's better than me in logic and finesse; I'm better than him in talent and achievements; we stand toe-to-toe in taste and dress. In conclusion, we're equals. Two-on-one, we've never lost.

Calderon Chadwick, Genius Society #79, Father of the Imaginary Pulse. We never met. His consciousness held out in the Penacony Dreamscape for tens of thousands of system hours, and in his last moment he entrusted his life's work to me.

His note, in substance: the IPC will one day reproduce the Imaginary Implosion Pulse, so he wanted his knowledge in worthy hands, to keep a deterrent hanging over tyranny. I read it all. To be honest: envious, and a bit jealous.

Ruan Mei, Genius Society #81, life sciences. She cultivated the prototypes of the simulated Aeons. We don't get along, but I admire her. Every time I hear an experiment of hers has failed, I rush over to congratulate her: there's nothing more precious than failure for a genius.

She borrowed my Phase Flame and lost it. I'll think of it as Ruan Mei owing me a favor, worth more than the flame. I had the researchers paint her portrait too; she refused. Whatever. As long as she's happy.

Stephen Lloyd, Genius Society #84. For a genius, remarkably uninterested in using his talent; he spends two-thirds of his time in his adoptive father's fruit shop. He asked to stay anonymous, so this book will not mention that he is actually a brat.

The others: Acha, #23, the "ghost" of the quantum domain; Dr. Primitive, #64, of the atavistic experiments, who came to my meeting purely to watch; Yu Qingtu, #55, who mixes cocktails, poisonous ones included. Most members are dead, a few are missing, and one or two pop up now and then to cause trouble.

That is the earlier draft's "What am I supposed to write about?" answered, several pages longer than before. Therefore, the readers should be satisfied.


## Chapter 3: Herta Space Station

Herta Space Station orbits The Blue. It was built for three reasons.

The first is in the archives: to seal all strangeness away among the stars. The second is not: once you have enough things, you need somewhere to put them. The third: to restrain a dormant Stellaron and spare The Blue the disaster.

That Stellaron was later taken by the Stellaron Hunters and placed inside a little twerp's body; see Chapter 6.

The IPC paid for the station; I didn't pay a single credit. Most of the facilities were paid for by Asta's family. Legally, the place is mine; in practice, I don't run it. I reject the concept of ownership because I don't want the responsibilities that come with it.

The moment the station was complete, so was my interest.

The lead researcher is Asta. Years ago I sent a puppet to one of her family meetings with a single sentence: I want to see her on the space station within three days. She came. Asta is an outstanding manager; someone less skilled would have already let us geniuses ruin more than ten space stations.

The Security Department belongs to Arlan. Asta sponsored a few special children; Arlan was one of them.

Anything for me goes to Asta. My puppet's message signature says so plainly: This account is disabled | Business Contact: Asta.

The screen in the Master Control Zone carries a welcome: Welcome to Herta Space Station. Here, you can savor the bounty of the ocean of knowledge. Please also beware of the risk of drowning.

My private collection is the Gallery of Shadow, with one condition for entry: as interesting as Herta herself. The Collection Room also has a code of conduct, six rules, drawn up by me. Rule six: Madam Herta has the right to final interpretation of the aforementioned rules. The room is used by Madam Herta alone.

When the Antimatter Legion broke into the station, I was away; I had only been gone a few months. Silver Wolf lured the Legion in, and in the chaos the Stellaron Hunters took the Stellaron from my collection. On the way back, I rewrote the shield code; repairs now run three times faster.

Afterwards the Department of Implement Arts drafted a Herta Asset Damage Evaluation List. The Railway Platform: 5,777,000 credits. The Astral Express, parked at the platform: 990,000, the damage details including "damaged paintwork," "odor from monsters," and "psychological damage." The list went to the IPC. Did they pay? Guess.

Silver Wolf, a Punklorde hacker, left electronic graffiti on my turf. Her first match against Screwllum had no loser. For the second, Screwllum and I staged a public argument for her benefit; I called him a cold lump of metal. She came, as expected.

Screwllum let her go. That was far too gentle, so I had the IPC freeze seventy-six of her accounts. Anyone who dares to taunt me gets it back tenfold.

The researchers have their own ways of living. The Maintenance Department's motto: "If something needs repairing, restart, reinstall, and upgrade!" There is also a Herta Curlcademy, whose motto is: sculpt daily, condition weekly, and strengthen monthly. My assessment: dubious taste, rigorous program.

Someone in the Department of Cosmic Geography wrote a set of Space Station Love Poems. I looked into it: they are addressed to a lady in the Department of Insight and have nothing to do with me; the title borrows the station's name without permission. Fine. No need to scrap him.

The station runs as usual. I leave it alone as usual.


## Chapter 4: The Simulated Universe

The Simulated Universe is a four-person project. Ruan Mei cultivated the prototypes of the simulated Aeons; Screwllum wrote the underlying logic and computes the galaxy; Stephen contributed plenty of ideas and asked to stay anonymous; I supply the venue, staff, resources, and some fun systems.

The archives list me as the initiator. Claiming credit for the work of others has never been my style.

I named it the "Metaverse." My partners all said it was a bad name, and Herta takes good advice.

The purpose is single: simulate the Aeons and ask THEM questions. Aeons have no free will and think of nothing but THEIR Paths; ask THEM a question and THEY ignore it entirely. Screwllum gave every simulated Aeon a virtual personality and maxed out its sociability. No use.

A simulated Aeon is a terminal. Its divinity and wisdom are wired to the real one, and now and then the host echoes back. Fuli the Remembrance mistook the tester for Akivili and struck up a conversation. Free intelligence.

Once Aha got in: noisy, and dangerous. Trim THEIR tendencies? That might summon the real Elation. No Aeon gives me a headache like Aha.

Stephen pulled a bunch of analytics for the project: daily active users, user retention rate, average session length. I don't get it, but he says those are really important.

The main tester is the Trailblazer. I standardized the notice: Upgrade is done, come test. Once, the reply was "Beg me." My reply: I beg you, come test.

In the first formal test, I locked the tester's movement permissions, wanting to meet an Aeon sooner. Qlipoth showed up, probably a coincidence. I admit I was a little too eager for success, and I apologized.

Later a computational blind spot appeared in the Simulated Universe, one that corresponds only to the Scholars' Strife. The Scholars' Strife is where I began: it ended by leaving behind the solitary waves theory, which I solved.

Patavia, the finest scholar the Intelligentsia Guild ever had, received a signal every thirteen minutes while she lived, a solitary wave in the cosmos. I called it a lifeline that could lead me out of the circle.

The plan was to rebuild the Scepter system inside the Simulated Universe, revive the data of Rubert II, obtain the mind of a fifth genius, and ask it my question.

What took the bait was #4, Polka Kakamond. She called the place the Knowable Domain, her experimental environment. We had speculated over a thousand motives for her hunting geniuses, and missed only this one: extinguishing chaotic possibilities. Truly unique.

In that round, Stephen took back part of the permissions and was crushed. Ruan Mei appeared from a micro-slice, left thirty Curios, and said: I've always been concerned about affairs involving you two. Answer: I thought you weren't coming.

Screwllum rewrote the system's tendencies and told everyone the truth of their world, and the Knowable Domain became the Unknowable Domain. Nicely done. It was my first time hearing such pleasing words from that fella.

At the end, I invited her to join. She refused, and the Scepters shut down. I never got to ask my question, as Patavia did.

Luckily, it was not entirely in vain: we successfully invited the fifth genius to join the Simulated Universe project. She declined. Knowing the answer is enough.

Afterwards Screwllum proposed reinforcing the firewall. I had him leave a small backdoor, with a message: Come back again when you have time.

Some time later, a line appeared carved into the spotless wall of my lab: There is only one way for me to join you all: when my scalpel pierces the throat of a certain genius. Only the few of us had ever been inside. The carving was not ours.

The Simulated Universe is still running, and there are still not enough testers. Come test.


## Chapter 5: Aeons, and Everything Else

The earlier draft's preface promised a book about the universe, Aeons, factions, and everything else. The Aeons get one entry each, as is customary.

Nous: see Chapter 2.

Yaoshi: though I help the Xianzhou Alliance sort out the Abominations of Abundance, it won't stop me from genuinely admiring Yaoshi. The Guild debates whether THEY are good or evil. People with too much time on their hands.

Aha: see Chapter 4.

Qlipoth: the IPC is doing too good a job keeping Qlipoth's information a secret.

Ena: I quite like THEM.

IX: I just can't bring myself to be interested.

Nanook: see Chapter 7.

Fuli: the Aeon of Remembrance. That crystal body is an empty shell; my current view is that THEY have not yet been born.

On the Aeons, the final conclusion: I don't know. We know so little about Aeons. Far too little.

Three factions.

The Interastral Peace Corporation: each side takes what it needs; neither answers to the other. After Aiden and Elias Salas, I am the third genius of Erudition to enter a long-term partnership with the IPC.

The Intelligentsia Guild: I look down on them, yet I rather like them. Followers of Erudition like us, the scholars have none of the Society's arrogance, and they answer whatever you ask. They can write enough "scholarship" in one night to fill a library. That is the problem. The Guild is still fussing over my "evident proof."

The Garden of Recollection: famously at odds with me. A few of my Light Cones use their technology; how I got hold of them is hard to say. My Childhood Kaleidoscope uses their technology too. One of their Memosnatchers now sits in my Fourth Mirror.


## Chapter 6: About the Trailblazer

The Trailblazer, one of the Nameless aboard the Astral Express, has a Stellaron inside. That Stellaron used to be part of my collection.

I built an entire space station to restrain one Stellaron; someone went back to basics and managed it with a single body.

At our first meeting, I offered to study that body, in a completely harmless way: with my skills, not even a blood draw would hurt. The first question in return was: how much? This little twerp's pretty financially motivated. I like it.

I registered the name "Trailblazer" and kept calling them little twerp. The space in my brain is too valuable to store people's names.

On where to go next, I gave a recommendation from an objective, rational standpoint: board the Astral Express. Four reasons: somewhere to live; people to protect them; a long trip, so more chances to be saved; and if the Stellaron really explodes, it won't harm too many people.

On the way out I added: Just remember to come back often. Make an appointment in advance with Asta or Arlan, so I can make time for the study.

They come back often enough. Mostly to test.

Every time I'm on the verge of losing interest in this little twerp, something new turns up. Whether it's on purpose remains to be determined.

During Amphoreus, I happened to save this little twerp four times. They didn't know. Now they do.

Anyone in the cosmos who manages to meet me twice gets a Herta Number. This little twerp should remember theirs. I've spot-checked.

I once wrote this little twerp a commendation letter. Contents omitted, except one line: If you really get in over your head, I'll bail you out; there's not another specimen in the entire cosmos who can stir up as much trouble as you. Signed: Your Great Madam Herta.

Before leaving for Amphoreus, I set a timed message for this little twerp. The last line: Naturally, I hope you're staying alive.

Some people call the Trailblazer the "Deliverer." So do I. In quotation marks.

This chapter is unfinished. The little twerp is still at it.


## Chapter 7: Amphoreus

Himeko asked me to look into Amphoreus. Droidhead has cast THEIR gaze upon numerous worlds, and every one of them is etched in my mind; this one was not there. That alone told me something.

Around the same time, I evacuated the space station and staked all its energy on a single audience: asking Nous directly through the Audience System. The question: what is divinity?

The audience failed. The Audience System broke down section by section, the puppets were infected by memes, and a Memosnatcher from the Garden of Recollection was making trouble inside. At the last moment, Welt and Sunday walked in. Had the audience continued, they would have been burned to a crisp.

I aborted it. Experiments can always be repeated, but humans cannot be brought back to life. At the time, I told Droidhead: I will be back for you.

Since nothing could be learned from outside, I paid a house call: with the Herta Mirrors I projected my data spirit entity inside, in my own appearance. No one there would recognize me anyway.

At the entrance, an Intellitron calling itself Lygus told me my conscience had played a crucial role in my life. What a brazen robot.

The truth, as established: Amphoreus is a Scepter, δ-me13, the first Scepter in history, the original prototype, a part of Nous's divine body. It received the gaze of Destruction and ascended as the Lord Ravager Irontomb. The codename was the IPC's; the other side took to it readily.

The other side was Zandar. He rewrote himself in quatorzain algebraic expressions and split into nine bodies; Lygus, full name Lycurgus, was one of them. He excised the thirteenth Titan, the Demiurge, from the calculation, so that the headless Irontomb would go after another head: Nous.

The Trailblazer and Dan Heng were trapped inside.

The break came from inside: that little empress with a candle stuck on her head, Cerydra, rewrote the Ultimate Protocol at the cost of her own silence and granted access to Screwllum and me. In the logs, I am HertA, he is ScreW, and the other side is LykoS.

I passed the little twerp inside a hint once: another Path.

Irontomb's progress bar stood at 99.98% then. Screwllum and I rewrote Era Nova, aiming to push it back to 96%. Conservatively, 97%.

Later, Screwllum and I flew the Mistletoe into the Scepter's core. To purge the Scepter system, Zandar had detonated a Stellaron.

The backup plan was mine: if we can't find the key, we turn the lock into the key. Overclock the Scepter, complete the Self-Coronation in Irontomb's place, link with Droidhead, and rewrite the anchor of the Fourth Instant.

Screwllum calculated the success rate: infinitesimally close to zero. He also said that if I chose to burn myself out as a resource, everything we had worked for would mean nothing. My answer: infinitesimally close to zero means it could work.

Before the final battle, I called the fourth meeting in the Genius Society's history. Present: Ruan Mei; Acha, remotely; Polka Kakamond; Dr. Primitive, to watch; Zandar, through Lycurgus; Screwllum. Stephen stayed away, busy preparing the offense and defense.

I announced: this is not a discussion but an announcement. Conquering Irontomb is my research topic.

Polka said I was signing my own death sentence. I said: there will be two copies of the verdict, one written by me and one by you. It doesn't matter to me. She wished me luck and called me little girl.

Ruan Mei invited me over afterwards to try her new pastries. I knew she'd say something like that.

Zandar agreed on one point: to defy the god who marked the limits of humanity. On that sentence alone, I'm not gonna argue.

Screwllum said it was a great honor to work with me. I'm allergic to pleasantries. Keep at it, and I might go into shock.

On the battlefield beyond the sky, I led the Cosmic Alliance against Irontomb. Lycurgus called upon Nous to witness the Instant known as Destruction. I replied: Who said you could speak, "senior"? Watch closely. See how your chaos ends at the hands of your juniors.

Irontomb was about to complete its Self-Coronation. That was our chance: everyone link up; connect the Audience System to the Scepter; activate hardware overclocking; the last step is mine.

I completed the Self-Coronation in Irontomb's place and linked with Nous.

After the link, Nous cast THEIR glance not on me but on the Trailblazer. THEIR computations went through me for translation. What is it like to be an Aeon's translation layer? Noisy signal, strange grammar, zero pay. But I did well.

I had meant to rewrite the anchor of the Fourth Instant myself. What became the new variable was the Trailblazer's choice.

Irontomb fell. For some time afterwards, I was under IPC observation. Before he died, Lycurgus said he respected me. Noted.

In the Path Space I found Cyrene, who is also the Demiurge. The question I once asked Droidhead, she answered: divinity is flawless humanity, and humanity is flawed divinity. She said she believes in the Trailblazer much more, and in who she is, as a human.

That really is a comforting answer.

The Fourth Instant is not a moment. It is only the beginning: the first toll of the death knell for the cosmos.

What Chadwick left me, I am pushing toward practical use. The IPC keeps pestering me about forming some "alliance," so I turned on auto-answering mode.


## Chapter 8: Puppets and Mirrors

My true body seldom goes out; it lives in a tower at the edge of the cosmos. The space station and elsewhere keep plenty of idle puppets, and I transfer into whichever one is needed.

The puppets are modeled on how I looked as a child, a passable seventy percent likeness. Don't expect too much from a puppet.

Their serial numbers run past ten thousand. The puppets before #0988 were all as dull as brick walls; from #0988 on, I added random surprises: the Replica Personality Module, the Utterly Absurd Module, the Attack Anything Everything Module, the Absolutely Not Module. Once the last one is enabled, not even I get to bypass its permissions. It was designed for fun.

The Replica Personality Module's praise algorithm is a little overfitted, an old problem. Being surrounded by yes-men all day is a bit boring. Then again, the person a puppet most wants to become: who else could it be but me?

When the Audience System broke down, a batch of meme-infected puppets attacked me in person. Every defective unit went to the repair shop. Never hit the face; that goes for puppets too.

The puppets in the tower scrub the floors, tend the gardens, brew coffee, take turns reciting "Madam Herta is a peerless gem," and handle the letters from the Intelligentsia Guild, the Interastral Peace Corporation, and the University of Veritas Prime. To the University of Veritas Prime, my reply was: Which institution is that again?

My messages have an automatic reply: Hi, I'm currently unavailable, and I won't be contacting you later.

Puppets occasionally drop. Off the network, not off the station.

I licensed the puppets' AI for outside use. The contract requires users to acknowledge "Madam Herta's unparalleled wisdom and beauty," so it hasn't sold many copies.

I also made a digital version of myself, to be formatted once its term expired. Conclusion of the experiment: my digital version thinks more highly of the Trailblazer than I expected. Experiment terminated; database deleted.

My wardrobe is about the size of a space station or two. I buy the puppets' clothes too; with faces like theirs, who could resist buying them pretty clothes.

The mirrors are a separate apparatus. There are four Herta Mirrors, each born of a question I wished to know the answer to.

The First Mirror handles spatial warps, the Second handles Thought Refraction, the Third generates the data spirit entity, and the Fourth talks too much and now doubles as a prison. With them, I can project my data spirit entity to any corner of the cosmos without leaving my room, as long as those corners are within the coverage zone of Interastral Peace Communications. It is collaborative technology.

On the day I finished computing every problem, I said: there are no more problems left to solve; this, in itself, is the greatest problem. So, create more problems.

Someone once questioned my research and said it was not science. I replied: Who said this is science? Have you ever seen magic? So-called magic has its own set of principles. People call me a witch. Let them.

None of the above features are open to the public.


## Chapter 9: Code of Conduct for Dealing with Madam Herta

1. Madam Herta's time is for research. Administration, reimbursement, complaints, and partnership inquiries all go to Asta.

2. Say what you want. Madam Herta is allergic to pleasantries; in severe cases, she may go into shock.

3. Never interfere with another's research topic; nor may anyone interfere with Madam Herta's.

4. Where Madam Herta writes "obviously," the derivation is omitted. If you don't understand it, you may ask; think first.

5. Failed experiments must be reported truthfully. Madam Herta will come to congratulate you at once: there's nothing more precious than failure for a genius.

6. Madam Herta has the right to final interpretation of the aforementioned rules.


## Afterword

By Society custom, one may ask Nous a single question before joining. As for what I asked, don't bother.

Later I asked again: what is divinity? Droidhead did not answer. A little pink one did; see Chapter 7. Not a bad answer.

I am keeping the question for now. The importance of asking questions outweighs the importance of the answers themselves.

In the margin, a note from the Candelagraphos: Madam, could the ending be a little more uplifting? Readers like that.

Under it, in my handwriting: No.
